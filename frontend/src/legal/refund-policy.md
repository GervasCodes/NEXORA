## 1. What this policy covers

This Refund Policy explains when you can get your money back for a product bought through
NEXORA, how to ask for it, how long you have, and how the refund is actually paid out. It
applies to Buyers and to the Sellers who fulfil their orders.

NEXORA is a **marketplace, not the merchant** — products are owned, priced, described, and
sold by independent Sellers (see the **[Terms of Service](/legal/terms-of-service)**,
Section 2). NEXORA administers the refund mechanism and decides disputes; the Seller is the
party whose earnings a refund is settled against.

**Nothing in this policy waives your rights under applicable consumer-protection law**,
which take precedence over anything here to the extent they conflict.

## 2. The two routes to a refund

There are two separate flows in the app, and which one you use depends on what went wrong:

| Route | Use it when | Where it lives in the app |
|---|---|---|
| **Cancellation** | The order hasn't shipped yet. | The order page, while the order is still `pending` or `processing`. |
| **Return** | The order arrived, but you want to send the item back. | "Request a return" on a delivered order. |
| **Dispute** | Something went wrong with the order — damaged, defective, wrong item, never arrived, or badly delayed. | "Report a problem" on the order. |

## 3. Cancelling before dispatch

You can cancel an order yourself while it is still in the **pending** or **processing**
state. Once the Seller marks it **shipped**, self-service cancellation is no longer
available and you should use the return or dispute route instead.

Where payment was already taken, a cancelled order is refunded under Section 6 below.

## 4. Returns

A return can only be opened on an order that has reached **delivered**, and only within the
return window for that order:

- **7 days** from the delivery date by default.
- **14 days** from the delivery date if the **buyer protection add-on** was purchased at
  checkout for that order.

Accepted return reasons are: damaged item, wrong item, defective product, not as described,
changed mind, and other.

A return moves through these states:

1. **Requested** — you open the return and give a reason (and, where relevant, photos).
2. **Approved** or **Rejected** — the Seller (or a NEXORA admin) reviews it. A rejection
   comes with a reason.
3. **Shipped back** — you send the item back to the Seller.
4. **Received** — the Seller confirms the item arrived.
5. **Refunded** — the refund is triggered automatically at this point, and any earnings
   already released to the Seller for the returned item(s) are reversed.

You can cancel your own return request while it is still **requested** or **approved**.

**Return shipping costs** are not automatically borne by NEXORA. Where a return is the
result of a Seller's fault (damaged on arrival, defective, wrong item, not as described),
raise it as part of the return or dispute record so it can be taken into account in the
outcome.

## 5. Disputes — when something went wrong

If an order arrived damaged, defective, wrong, badly delayed, or never arrived at all, use
**Report a problem** on the order to open a dispute. Dispute types are: damaged item,
delayed delivery, defective product, wrong item, missing delivery, and other.

Both you and the Seller can exchange messages and evidence on the case. A NEXORA admin
reviews it and issues one of:

- **Full refund**
- **Partial refund**
- **Replacement**
- **Compensation**
- **No action** (rejected, with a stated reason)

Who is held responsible for what — Seller, Delivery Agent, or neither — is set out in the
**[Delivery Liability Policy](/legal/delivery-liability-policy)**, which also covers the
evidence expected from each party. Report problems **as soon as possible**, and in any case
within the window shown in the app for that order.

Opening a dispute in bad faith, or repeatedly filing unfounded disputes, is a violation of
the Terms of Service and may result in account restrictions.

## 6. How a refund is actually paid

This is the part most likely to differ from what you may expect from a single-merchant
online shop, so it is stated plainly:

- Refunds are settled by **reversing the Seller's wallet earnings** for the affected
  amount — the same ledger used for Seller withdrawals.
- Where fault sits with a **Delivery Agent** rather than the Seller, the Seller is made
  whole through that Agent's own arrangement with them. Delivery Agents are engaged by
  Sellers, not by NEXORA; NEXORA's fault attribution and dispute history exist to give
  both parties evidence for that settlement.
- **Returning money to your original payment method is not fully automated across every
  payment provider today.** For some payment rails a NEXORA admin completes the payout
  manually, outside the app. Expect this to take longer than the in-app resolution itself,
  and keep the dispute or return record open as your reference.

Where a refund is credited to your **NEXORA wallet** instead, that balance can be used for
future orders or withdrawn through the normal withdrawal flow.

## 7. Coupons, loyalty points, and add-ons

- A **coupon discount** applied to an order is not refunded as cash; a refund returns what
  was actually paid.
- **Loyalty points** redeemed against a refunded order are handled as part of the refund
  record — raise it in the dispute or return thread if points were burned on an order that
  was subsequently refunded.
- The **buyer protection add-on fee**, where purchased, buys the extended 14-day return
  window described in Section 4 and the cover described in the
  **[Insurance Policy](/legal/insurance-policy)**.

## 8. Services and bookings

Bookings for services follow the provider's own cancellation terms as shown on the service
at the time of booking, and exit through **completed**, **cancelled**, or **refunded**
states rather than a delivery step. A booking that is refunded is settled against the
provider the same way a product refund is settled against a Seller.

## 9. Sellers' obligations

Under the **[Vendor Agreement](/legal/vendor-agreement)**, Sellers are responsible for the
quality, safety, and accuracy of description of what they list, and must accept returns or
provide a remedy per that agreement's defect-handling clause. Refusing a legitimate return
or repeatedly losing disputes is grounds for account action against a Seller.
